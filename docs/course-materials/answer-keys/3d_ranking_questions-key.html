Work the colab through with your partner first, then come here. The code below is one correct answer, not the only one. Part 1: Filtering

1. How many rows describe units whose unit_type is exactly National Park?

Code 
national_parks = parks[parks['unit_type'] == 'National Park']
national_parks.shape
(4743, 12)
Code 
national_parks['unit_name'].nunique()
61
✅ Answer

4,743 rows, but only 61 National Parks. A row is one park in one year, not one park, and the file covers 113 years of records. If you wrote “there are 4,743 National Parks” you read the row count as a count of things in the world, which it is not.

National Park is also only one of 30 values in unit_type. Most of the file is National Monuments, Historic Sites and the rest of the National Park Service, so this filter throws away about four fifths of the table.

2. How many rows have a year of Total? Use ~ or != to build a table called by_year that has none of them in it. How many rows does by_year have?

Code 
totals = parks[parks['year'] == 'Total']
print(totals.shape)

by_year = parks[~(parks['year'] == 'Total')]
print(by_year.shape)
(386, 12)
(21174, 12)
✅ Answer

386 Total rows, leaving 21,174 rows in by_year. The number 386 is not arbitrary: it is exactly the number of distinct units in the file, so every unit has one and only one Total row.

Those rows are sums of the other rows in the same table. Great Smoky Mountains has a Total of 521,947,058, and adding up its 113 yearly values gives 521,947,058 as well. Leaving them in means counting every visitor twice, once in the year they visited and once in the lifetime sum. Every ranking in Parts 2 and 3 depends on having removed them first.

The distinction matters because only four parks ever did it. Great Smoky Mountains accounts for 55 of the 59 rows, Grand Canyon for two (2015 and 2016), Yosemite for one (2016) and Acadia for one (1989). A row count of 59 sounds like a broad group and is in fact one park repeated.

Keep that in mind before treating any single year as a fact about the world.

The other 39 are much smaller than the top 10 table suggests. The median park recorded 607,479 visitors, and the smallest, Gates of the Arctic in Alaska, recorded 10,047. Any sentence that begins “national parks are crowded” is really a sentence about roughly twenty of them.

Great Smoky Mountains National Park, with 11,312,786 visitors, which is 1.9 times Grand Canyon’s 5,969,811 and 13.5% of all National Park visits that year. The gap between first and second place is larger than second place itself.

.idxmax() returned the index label of the largest row, not the value. That is exactly why it is paired with .loc[]: one step answers “which one?” and the next answers “how much?”, and neither step requires you to look at a table with your eyes.

✅ A model answer

Before I can answer I need three things settled. First, which units count: only the 61 National Parks, or all 386 National Park Service units? Second, which year, or whether they mean the all-time total. Third, whether the Total rows have been removed, because leaving them in makes every answer a lifetime sum.

If they mean National Parks in 2016, the answer is Great Smoky Mountains with 11,312,786 visitors, nearly twice Grand Canyon’s 5,969,811. If they mean all Park Service units in 2016, Great Smoky Mountains is third: Golden Gate National Recreation Area recorded 15,638,777 and Blue Ridge Parkway 15,175,578. If they mean the busiest single year ever recorded, it is Golden Gate in 1987 at 21,767,176.

So the answer I would give is: for National Parks, Great Smoky Mountains, and the margin is large. But if they are asking because they want to know where the crowds are, the parkways and recreation areas beat every National Park, and I would want to know what they are planning to do with the number before I picked one.

Where the marks are

If you compare your notebook against this key, look for these four things before you look at anything else.

  1. Your by_year has 21,174 rows, and you built it before you ranked anything. Every wrong answer in Part 2 traces back to the 386 Total rows.
  2. Your answer to question 6 names a unit that is not a National Park. Golden Gate National Recreation Area in 1987 is the correct answer. If you wrote a National Park, you filtered when the question did not ask you to.
  3. You compared year to the string '2016', with quotes. The column is text. Comparing it to the integer 2016 returns zero rows and raises no error, which makes it one of the hardest mistakes in pandas to notice.
  4. Your answers say what a row is. 4,743 rows is not 4,743 parks, and 59 rows above 5 million is four parks, not 59. Every count in this file is a count of park-years until you say otherwise.
